# World layout and rendering

The inhabited district is **29.25 × 22.2 world units**. The original footprint was 19.5 × 14.8, so this is 1.5× on each horizontal axis and 2.25× the area. A continuous 1,020 × 1,020 scenic terrain now surrounds it. Capacity remains eight residents: two in each zone. Both the simulator and live mode use the same scenery and seat geometry.

The wider camera-travel area now has continuous biome influence rather than one uniform meadow: Western Woods around X = −83, Z = −20, cooler Northern Highlands beyond Z ≈ −85, and warm Amber Foothills around X = −104, Z = 88. The forest adds deterministic instanced trees, while highland groundcover becomes sparse and stony and Amber groundcover gains warmer flowers and rocks. Blending uses world coordinates, so color and detail do not jump at the 60-unit detail-chunk boundaries. The broad biomes are scenic context; Lantern Hollow is an authored resident destination inside the Western Woods. The rest of the terrain is not a general walkable surface.

## Layout contract

`src/world/layout.ts` owns the original district footprint, rectangular floor bounds, eight seat origins, and fifteen camera presets. X is left/right, Y is elevation, and Z is depth. The grass surface is Y = 0.4. Floor coordinates specify the top surface rather than the center of a mesh. The surrounding terrain is mostly scenic. `src/world/terrain.ts` supplies both the smooth design height and the exact triangle-interpolated height of the rendered 1,020 × 1,020, 216-segment mesh. Most outside routes and visible ribbons use a walkway height above the higher of those surfaces. The Lantern Hollow spur follows the rendered triangles directly: resident nodes sit 0.105 units above them and its visible ribbon 0.08 units above them.

Café steps and studio stairs connect their raised floor edges. An authored waypoint network connects resident routes to shared destinations across the diorama and beyond it. Stair nodes encode elevation changes; there is no generated collision mesh.

Each seat anchors its desk, laptop, chair, and a reversible local character route. The route enters 1.1 units to the right and 2.1 behind the seat, pauses at a resting spot, then approaches the chair from the side. Preserve body clearance around the entire route when placing furniture. Residents can continue from their resting spots onto the cross-zone graph. See [Resident identity and journeys](resident-journeys.md) for destination reservations and interruption behavior. Tests check route/body margins and desk bounds against each floor; visual review is still needed for furniture intersections.

## How detail selection works

`DetailLayer.tsx` evaluates each zone every 0.2 seconds:

1. Test a conservative zone bounding sphere against the camera frustum.
2. Estimate screen size from the active camera. Orthographic zoom is CSS pixels per world unit; in Horizon perspective, distance also changes apparent size.
3. On high quality, mount fine detail at 26 pixels/unit. Keep it until scale falls below 22, preventing flicker near the threshold.
4. Unmount it when offscreen or when low quality is selected.

Fine layers include floor seams, counter fluting, espresso/bakery props, small signs, books, vines, flowers, and steam. Their scene objects and frame callbacks unmount with the layer. Meadow hummocks, flowers, and stones use a separate spatial-chunk selector. Seven authored landmark regions also mount nearby décor with a measured camera footprint, immediate destination prefetch, and unload hysteresis; Horizon shows them all. Their routes and arrival ground remain continuous, as do Southwind Mere and the far ridge. See [Spatial scenery chunks](spatial-streaming.md). The home district, desks, residents, and navigation state remain mounted. Three.js additionally skips drawing individual meshes outside the view. Shared surface textures remain loaded for the base scene; this is not whole-zone memory eviction.

Low quality also uses DPR 1 and 512-pixel shadows; high quality allows DPR up to 1.6 and 2048-pixel shadows. A local diagnostic overlay is available with `?renderStats=1`; see [Rendering performance](rendering-performance.md). No device frame-rate guarantee has been established. Screen-space detail selection reduces optional geometry but does not eliminate the costs of the base scene, resident animation, shadows, or the existing Three.js bundle.

## Agent state is independent

Fine-detail selection never wraps bridge connections, Zustand state, resident assignment, or resident animation. Zooming away does not mark a session idle, hide it from the list, or stop receiving events. The latest-eight/pinned seating policy is unchanged. No provider jobs are launched by entering a zone.

## Extending the world

Change layout coordinates first, then update navigation nodes/edges and destination clearances, place scenery around the routes, and retune camera spans. Keep useful silhouettes in the base layer and optional decoration in a fine layer. Keep per-frame animation on mutable Three.js objects, avoid React state on every frame, and dispose textures/materials owned by a component.

Before adding many more districts, profile frame time, draw calls, shadow cost, and memory on representative devices. Meadow groundcover now uses spatial ownership and shared instanced geometry. Actual asset streaming would additionally need asynchronous loading and prefetch/eviction boundaries. Infinite procedural terrain remains future work. The five northern and western landmarks, two trail rests, southern mere, glasshouse, and mill are connected destinations, not a general walkable landscape. See the [world expansion plan](world-expansion-plan.md) for the staged path to more traversable districts.

Validation: `npm test`, `npm run build`, then inspect overview and each zone, high/low quality, evening, reduced motion, follow/watch, and narrow viewports. Automated layout/detail tests live in `src/world/layout.test.ts`.
